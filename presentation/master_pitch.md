# Round 1 Master Pitch

Chleo, your firm receives customer complaints that have to be reviewed and routed to the right team for action. Today, that is a largely manual process, and it gives us a useful, low-risk way to test whether AI can create real operational value for a firm like yours.

The idea is not to let AI handle complaints or make decisions for your staff. It is simply to give your complaint handlers an assistant that reads each complaint, suggests which team should handle it, and shows the customer’s own words that support its recommendation. Your staff remain responsible for the final decision.

Before asking you to test this on your own complaints, I wanted to establish that the approach was credible enough to take further. On 60 public complaints from the U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, the assistant made 52 routing proposals and stopped on 8 cases when its checks failed rather than forcing an answer. A larger 240-case test also showed that the system can identify meaningful routing patterns, but the available labels were selected by consumers rather than expert complaint handlers, so they are not a reliable basis for judging whether the system is ready for use.

That is why the next test needs to happen with your data and your people. We would ask two of your experienced complaint handlers to label a representative sample of 300 historical complaints—large enough to give us a reliable first measure of performance, while keeping the burden on your team proportionate. We would then compare the assistant’s recommendations against decisions you actually trust and, if the results justify it, run a 60-day shadow pilot. During that period, the system can make recommendations, but nobody will act on them.

So what I am asking you to approve today is a controlled pilot to determine, with your own data and your own people, whether this is worth pursuing.
